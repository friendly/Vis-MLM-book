# Vis-MLM Jokes:
  
  
  1. Why did the scatterplot go to therapy? It felt its life lacked a sufficient visual summary — so the therapist drew an ellipse around it and said, "There. That's all of you."


2. A univariate statistician walks into a bar. Then another one does. Then another. The multivariate statistician just says, "Gentlemen, cbind() yourselves and we'll test you all at once."


3. My PCA juicer promised to squeeze all the information out of 47 dimensions into two. The pulp went into the third eigenvalue, and nobody's seen it since.


4. HE plot dating advice: if your Hypothesis ellipse doesn't poke outside the Error ellipse, they're just not that into you.


5. A Flatlander asked me what a 6-dimensional ellipsoid looks like. I said, "Exactly like an ellipse, if you squint in five directions."


6. Why are ridge trace plots so calm? Because they've learned to shrink their problems.


7. Collinearity is when two predictors are so close they finish each other's sentences — and the variance inflation factor sends you the bill.


8. Box's M walked up to the groups and asked, "Are your covariance matrices all equal?" Every one of them lied, and Box's M rejected them all anyway.


9. The Palmer Penguins tried to hide their species differences, but canonical discriminant analysis found the one direction where they couldn't help waddling apart.


10. Base R's anova() only does Type I tests. It believes whoever entered the model first gets to keep the sum of squares. car::Anova() calls this "sequential privilege."


11. The 1900–1950 Modern Dark Ages of data visualization weren't really dark. Everyone was just reading tables — like extracting sunlight from a cucumber.


12. I asked my MLM how it was feeling. It said, "Overall, significant — but I'd rather not say along which dimension."
